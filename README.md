# Hi, I'm Susie Göbel

I'm a computer scientist, researcher and lecturer at the **University of Koblenz**, Germany, currently working towards my PhD.

My interests lie at the intersection of software engineering, model-driven engineering and human-AI interaction. I enjoy exploring how models, tools and abstractions can make software development more accessible, structured and flexible.

## 🪷 A Little About Me

My path into computer science and academia has never been particularly linear – and I'm quite happy about that.

I graduated from the University of Kaiserslautern in 2014, shortly after becoming a mother. Since then, I've worn quite a few different hats: researcher, school teacher, software engineer and, eventually, CTO of a Swiss software company. I spent several years in industry, working part-time while taking on the challenges and responsibilities of a leadership role.

After years of balancing professional responsibilities and family life, I eventually decided to take a step back, rethink my priorities and return to academia – to pursue the research and teaching that I have always loved.

I believe there is more than one way to build a successful career, particularly for women in technology. Mine certainly hasn't followed the conventional path, and I wouldn't want it to. I've made choices that mattered to me, sometimes at the expense of professional opportunities, and I consider that a strength rather than a limitation.

**Being ambitious doesn't mean having to sacrifice everything else. And success doesn't have to look the same for everyone.**

## 🛠️ Projects

### [EMFular](https://github.com/softlang/EMFular)
A TypeScript-based framework for developing graphical and textual, model-driven editors.

EMFular aims to bring concepts and ideas from the Eclipse Modeling Framework (EMF) into the modern web development ecosystem.


### [KEML](https://github.com/keml-group)
Finding relevant information pieces and assessing their trust-worthiness in multi-turn LLM conversations.

This web-based [modeling tool](https://keml-group.github.io/web-editor/) allows user to visualize their LLM conversations and find agreement and contradictions within the received information.

## 🎓 Teaching

I teach software engineering and web application development, including topics such as:

- Web-based Modeling
- Software Language Engineering
- Compiler Construction
- Program Language Theory

I particularly enjoy project-based teaching, where students develop their own applications and explore different ways of solving the same problem.
Though this type of course is often more work (for both sides!) than classical lectures, it is also usually more approachable for students and makes them truly understand a concept they otherwise mostly recite.
Also, they can achieve a higher confidence in the practical work that most of them will do after university.
